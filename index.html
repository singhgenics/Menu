laying a place

What is on the table before the first plate arrives. Forks and spoons sit in a cutlery cup on each table.

  1. Side plate, 7.5". Eat from it, and set a steamer basket on it when dim sum arrives.
  2. Rice bowl. Rice, noodles, curry and gravy are served into it from the sharing vessel; jasmine rice comes in it.
  3. Dipping dish. Soy and chilli vinegar to start; swapped for black vinegar or kasundi mayo as the dish needs.
  4. Porcelain soup spoon. For every soup.
  5. Chopsticks on a ceramic rest. Reusable, square-edged so they don't roll.
  6. Water glass, filled from a table carafe, and a folded cotton napkin.
  7. Per table: one condiment set, a cutlery cup and coasters.

How it leaves the pass

plating

One card per menu item: the target photo, the vessel, the build order, garnish, serving temperature and a kitchen note. Photos are visual targets made for this guide; the chef sets final portion weights at the first tasting.

six rules for the pass

Warm the hot, chill the cold.Hot plates and bowls rinsed in hot water. Beer glasses, shots and coupes come from the fridge or freezer.
Build off-centre, low and loose.Heap wok food in a low mound with the rim showing. Air under fried food keeps it crisp.
Wipe every rim.A clean rim is the cheapest finish. Wipe plates, bowls, glasses and sauce dishes before they leave.
Sauces on the side, in their own dish.Dipping dishes carry sauces so fried and steamed food stays dry.
Garnish last, and only what is on the card.Herbs, sesame, chilli and crumbs go on in the final 10 seconds so they stay bright and crisp.
Out within a minute.Fried and wok food waits no longer than 60 seconds on the pass. Steam leaves lidded; the lid comes off at the table.
