What I would change

Based on the crockery list and your two additions: sauces on the table and free cheeselings.

1

Cheeselings arrive with the guests, not before them

Do: keep them in an airtight jar at the pass and bring a small bowl to the table as the guests sit, with the water. Why: Goa's humidity can soften a baked cheese snack quickly, and a bowl left out all morning is wasted. Vessel: the 14 cm enamel bowl you already list for wings and shells, one per table, refilled once on request. Add a bowl count of 10 plus spares to the buying list.

2

Give the cheeselings a cost and an allergen line before you print the menu

Do: cost a handful (about 25 to 30 g per table is my guess, not a measured figure) against your recipe, and add them to the allergen line. They normally contain gluten and milk, often egg, so say so on the menu and tell the team to mention it. Check: the cheese and its rennet if you want them labelled vegetarian, and whether you bake them in house or buy them in.

3

Keep the sauce caddy, but make it a proper station

Do: one caddy per table with three glass cruets that have steel pour spouts: soy, green chillies in vinegar, and chilli oil. Give the chilli oil a small spoon or a dropper top, because the crisp part sinks. Put a tiny printed tag on each cruet. Why: open pots go sticky and attract flies near the garden doors. Gochujang mayo, kasundi and black vinegar stay in the kitchen and arrive in the dipping dish with the dish they belong to.

4

Chilli oil needs an allergen note

Do: confirm what is in your chilli oil. Many include sesame and some include peanuts. If yours does, put it on the caddy tag and on the menu. Soy also contains gluten and soy.

5

The 0.6 × 0.6 m sofa tables cannot carry the full centre set

Why: on your floor plan the tables for 2 are 0.6 m square. Two covers take most of the width, and the strip left between the plates is about 17 cm wide and 60 cm long, which has to hold the dishes when they arrive. Do: on tables for 2, keep only the sauce caddy and, at seating, the cheeselings bowl. Leave off the carafe and the cutlery cup: pour water from a jug at the server station, and bring forks and spoons on request. Take the cheeselings bowl away once the first dish lands. For the 1.2 × 0.65 m tables for 4: the strip is about 22 cm, and the four centre pieces fit end to end, but check it with the real pieces on one table before you order.

6

Move the napkin stripe from olive to cabin red

Why: the list still says oatmeal khadi with an olive stripe, from the old look. With the Cabin East kit the red is the colour people will recognise from the sign. Do: ask the khadi maker for a red stripe, and print the coasters in the same red with the roof-and-wok mark.

7

Keep the empty rice bowl stacked on the side plate

Why: it saves table space at every cover and looks tidier. The chopsticks lie to the right of the plate on their rest, and the glass sits on its coaster beyond them. Do not pre-set a second plate; the steamer basket sits on the side plate when dim sum arrives.

Opening routine and turning a table

Set up, 30 minutes before opening

  1. Wipe the table and the edge, then dry it.
  2. Coasters down first, one per cover, where the glass will stand.
  3. Side plate in front of each seat, rice bowl stacked on top, dip dish at the top right.
  4. Chopsticks on the rest to the right of the plate, napkin folded to the left with the soup spoon beside it.
  5. Water glass on its coaster, carafe filled with chilled water in the centre.
  6. Caddy: pourers clean, each cruet filled to the same line, tags facing the guests.
  7. Cutlery cup with forks and spoons standing in it.
  8. Stand at each seat and look at the table once. Fix anything crooked.

When guests sit

  • Bring the cheeseling bowl and say what they are, and that they are free.
  • Pour the first water.
  • Offer the menu and mention the sauces on the table.

Turning the table

  • Clear everything, including the caddy and the cheeseling bowl.
  • Wipe the cruet necks, top up, and check the chilli oil spoon.
  • Reset the covers exactly as above. Never reuse a napkin.
